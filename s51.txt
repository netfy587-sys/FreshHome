    <div>
      <h2>Where the effort actually goes</h2>
      <p>A road bike converts leg power into forward motion through a chain of very simple mechanisms — but small changes in any of them change how a ride feels enormously. Tire pressure, gear choice, and body position all move the needle more than most riders expect.</p>
      <p>Cadence — how fast the pedals turn — matters as much as gear choice. Most efficient riders hold somewhere around 85 to 95 revolutions per minute, adjusting gears rather than slowing their spin on hills.</p>

<section id="etiquette">
  <div class="wrap">
    <h2>Riding in a group</h2>
    <ul class="eti-list">
      <li><span class="name">Hold your line</span><p>Predictable positioning matters more in a group than individual speed — sudden swerves cause the crashes.</p></li>
      <li><span class="name">Call out hazards</span><p>Potholes, gravel, and cars are usually announced verbally and with a hand signal to riders behind.</p></li>
      <li><span class="name">Take a fair turn</span><p>Riders at the front rotate through, sharing the work of blocking wind for the group.</p></li>
